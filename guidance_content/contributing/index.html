    Posting updates & contributing

    Pilots publish their progress on this Hub, ask questions, compare approaches with other pilots, and suggest additions to the Knowledge Base. All of it happens through GitHub Discussions on the Hub's repository.

    • Post a pilot update

      Progress, milestones, lessons, assets and events — published on your pilot's page and in the updates feed.

      How to post

    • Ask and compare

      Questions on building LDTs, and shared challenges where each pilot brings its own experience.

      Q&A and peer exchange

    • Suggest to the Knowledge Base

      Propose a resource, an organisation, or a value for one of the lists.

      Suggestions

    • Share an asset or service

      Components, configurations and deployment recipes other communities can reuse.

      Assets & Services Repository

    Posting a pilot update

    There are two routes. They end up in the same place, so use whichever suits how your pilot works.

    No repository needed. Open the Pilot updates category and start a new discussion. A form guides you through the fields.

    Register your GitHub usernames first

    Everyone who posts needs a GitHub account, and their username must be registered for your pilot by the LDT4SSC team. Only posts from registered members are published.

    Send the usernames of everyone who will post to the LDT4SSC team before you start.

    What the form asks for

    Field
    Title 5–200 characters, written above the form
    Pilot your pilot, from the list
    Type of update General · Milestone · Lesson · Asset · Event
    Work strand WS1, WS2 or WS3
    Update the text itself — formatted, and you can drag images straight in
    Authors one per line, as Name, Organisation
    Date optional; defaults to the day you post
    Tags optional; lowercase, comma-separated
    Related links optional; repositories, documents or datasets, one per line
    Event name, location, link for events only
    Which type to choose
    • General — news.
    • Milestone — a step reached.
    • Lesson — something learned.
    • Asset — something you produced: data, code, a document.
    • Event — something you held or attended.

    To change an update, edit the discussion. To withdraw it, delete the discussion.

    If your pilot works from the pilot repository template, write updates as Markdown files alongside your code, on GitHub or GitLab.

    Where the file goes

    • in the updates/ folder;
    • named YYYY-MM-DD-short-title.md — the date, then lowercase words separated by hyphens.

    What goes in the front matter

    Follow update-v1.schema.json. Required:

    Field Value
    schema_version 1
    type general, milestone, lesson, asset or event
    date YYYY-MM-DD
    title 5–200 characters
    work_strand WS1, WS2 or WS3
    authors each with name and organisation

    Optional: tags, related_assets, and related_event for events.

    Example 
    ---
schema_version: 1
type: milestone
date: 2026-10-21
title: First cross-border data exchange completed
work_strand: WS1
authors:
  - name: Maria Rossi
    organisation: City of Turin
  - name: Jan de Vries
    organisation: TNO
tags:
  - data-spaces
  - interoperability
---

Our two context brokers exchanged their first entities through the data space
connector this week…

    The template's check — the Hub's validate-updates workflow — runs on your commits and reports anything that does not match the schema, so you find problems before publication.

    What happens next

    Whichever route you take:

    1. Updates are collected every 6 hours by the Hub's aggregator.
    2. They appear on your pilot's page under Pilot Journey, in the updates feed, and in the RSS feed.
    3. Every update has a discussion in the Pilot updates category. Replies there appear as comments under the update on the Hub.

    Commenting needs a GitHub account, but reading does not.

    Asking and comparing

    Open to anyone with a GitHub account, not only pilots.

    • Q&A

      Questions on building Local Digital Twins, the LDT4SSC methodology, or the open calls. The form asks for a topic — technical, data governance and legal, methodology, open calls and administration, or other — an optional work strand, and what you are trying to do and have already tried.

      Ask a question

    • Peer exchange

      For a challenge several pilots are likely to share. You describe the topic and its context, say what you would like to hear from others — approaches, tools, pitfalls, examples from other cities — and each pilot replies with its own experience.

      Open a topic

    Suggesting to the Knowledge Base

    The Knowledge Base is the linked catalogue behind this Hub, covering resources, organisations, pilots and their updates, and Hub pages.

    Anyone can suggest additions. Others upvote or comment, and curators add agreed suggestions.

    You can suggest a resource (standard, tool, guideline, report), an organisation, or a value for one of the lists (resource type, layer, building block). The form asks for a main link, a description in your own words, why it matters for Local Digital Twins, the publisher, and who it is for.

    Suggest an addition

    To report an error in an existing entry

    Open an issue on the repository rather than a suggestion.

    Sharing assets and services

    Components you build are expected to return to the community — containers, configurations, deployment recipes, data models and documentation that another community can pick up.

    The Assets & Services Repository is under development

    The repository is being built and is not open for submissions yet. The LDT4SSC team will announce it in Announcements once pilots can submit to it.

    In the meantime, package as you go rather than leaving it to the end of your pilot, and post an Asset update when you produce something — that way the work is visible, and migrating it into the repository later is straightforward.

    What to prepare

    • components packaged as containers, with Helm charts and API specifications where they apply;
    • configuration samples for different contexts, so adopters are not reverse-engineering yours;
    • the documentation that makes reuse possible — data requirements, deployment procedure, governance arrangements, and for AI services a model card;
    • an open licence.

    Some outputs have destinations of their own already: data models go to Smart Data Models, and services to the EU LDT Marketplace.

    Where each kind of output goes

    All discussion categories

    Category For
    Pilot updates Pilots posting progress, milestones, lessons, assets and events
    Peer exchange A challenge several pilots share, with each bringing its experience
    Q&A Questions on building LDTs, the methodology, the open calls
    Knowledge Base suggestions Proposing a resource, organisation or vocabulary value
    Announcements Open calls, events and releases from the LDT4SSC team
    Polls Quick consultations

    Documenting on the Hub is a requirement

    Keeping your pilot's documentation up to date is binding — Rq23 in WS1, Rq31 in WS2 and WS3. Posting updates is how that requirement is met in practice.
